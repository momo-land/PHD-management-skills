# Academic translation and editing

## Identify the language task

Use the supplied text, target language, field terminology and purpose. If editing intensity is unspecified, make conservative changes that preserve the argument.
Ask only when the language direction or a meaning ambiguity affects the result. Preserve determinate numbers, units and conclusions.

## Transform text without changing meaning

1. Extract key terms, proper names, abbreviations and established translations into a short glossary and apply it consistently.
2. Identify subjects, comparisons, logical relationships, conditions and uncertainty before translating each passage.
3. Preserve sample sizes, values, intervals, symbols, equations, units, citation keys and figure references; formatting must not change magnitude or direction.
4. Retain evidential strength such as may, suggest and associated. Do not turn possibility into certainty or association into causation.
5. Offer alternatives for ambiguous sentences with their meaning differences instead of silently choosing a scientific conclusion.
6. Improve syntax and paragraph flow without adding references, results or stronger contribution claims.
7. Compare the output against the original for terminology, numbers, negation, conditions, tense and citations. Track coverage when processing long texts in sections.

## Deliver the requested form

Provide a full translation, bilingual comparison or edited draft as requested, with brief explanations of changes affecting meaning.
Avoid explaining every routine grammar edit; separate ambiguities that require the author's judgment.
Do not promise AI-detector evasion or acceptance, and do not treat fluent language as proof of scientific validity.

## Format and state boundaries

- With readable text alone, deliver text. State when Word revision handling or LaTeX compilation has not been validated.
- Preserve Markdown/LaTeX commands, citation keys and equations as structure. Apply requested terminology changes across the accessible scope and identify any remaining scope.
- A language preference or interface-language change does not authorize translation of stored titles, quotations or source material.
- Save a requested new draft to the chosen location or outputs/. Edit originals within the task's authorization; a translation-only request produces a new draft and does not change paper percentages.
- Use [paper writing](../phd-paper/SKILL.en.md) for substantive argument revision, still constrained by the available evidence.

## Workspace synchronization (only when requested)

Read the [local workflow](../phd-manager/references/local-workflow.en.md), locate the actual repository and project, and preserve the current PHD_DATA_DIR.
Discussion, translation, drafts and suggestions do not automatically change research state. Deliver requested files to the selected directory, or outputs/ when unspecified.
For requested saves, use the CLI to read the target and current version; preserve notes, relations and user-estimated percentages rather than editing the database directly.
Use user for user facts, import for external material and ai for AI plans/assessments, preserving provenance and actual verification scope.
Validate a batch before applying multiple changes and read back successful writes before reporting them as saved. Add no fields outside the contract.

## Imported upstream resources

When the task benefits from the original methods or bundled tools, use the [GitHub integration](../github-humanizer/SKILL.en.md) and read its complete upstream instructions. Check its requirements and scope; tool output does not replace the local record workflow.
